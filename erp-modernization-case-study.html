Context

Premier's ERP powers purchasing, requisitioning, approvals and invoicing for healthcare organizations. It works, but it looked and behaved like software from another era.
Over years of growth, screens had been built by different teams at different times. The same action could look different from one module to the next. Grids, filters, forms and page headers each had their own variations, so every new screen meant re-learning the interface.
Meanwhile, Premier had introduced the Premier Design System as the shared foundation for its product portfolio. The ERP needed to catch up. It needed a modern look and feel, consistent with the rest of Premier's products.

The challenge

Two goals ran in parallel: modernize what already existed, and extend the platform into a new purchasing category.
Goal 01

Modernize

Give the ERP a fresh, modern look and feel aligned to the Premier Design System.

Goal 02

Standardize

Replace one-off screens with shared components, page templates and workflow patterns.

Goal 03

Extend

Add pharmacy capabilities so customers could purchase and manage pharmacological products.

How might we make a dense, decades-deep ERP feel like one coherent, modern product without losing the power its users depend on?

Design principles

Four principles kept a large, multi-workflow redesign consistent.
01

One pattern per job

A grid, a filter bar or a details page should behave the same everywhere it appears.

02

Build from the system

Every screen is assembled from Premier Design System components, not custom one-offs.

03

Keep the power, lose the clutter

Preserve the data users depend on while giving it clear hierarchy and room to breathe.

04

Design every state

Editing, empty results, loading and errors are part of the workflow, not afterthoughts.

The redesigned workflows

Each workflow shares the same frame: a persistent application rail, a collapsible module navigation, a consistent page header, and a standard filter bar.
Workflow 01

Dashboard

The new home screen gives quick access to the most-used workflows and surfaces the numbers people check daily: approvals, issues, items on order and items in cart. Configurable KPI cards replace scattered reports.

Workflow 02

Shop

Shopping moved from a raw item lookup to a familiar catalog experience. Users can switch between a visual tile view and a dense list view. Supplier punchout to partners like Henry Schein, Staples, Office Depot and Grainger sits right in the page, alongside a path for non-file items.

Workflow 03

Requisitions

Requisition lines now use the standard data grid, with column filters, row actions and quick icons for viewing, attachments and notes. Requisition details follow one template: a summary up top, then Details, Notes and Documents tabs.

Workflow 04

Approvals

Approvers work from one queue split into requisitions and invoices. Invoice details bring header information, matched lines and distribution lines onto a single page, with approve and reject actions always in reach.

Adding pharmacy

Alongside the modernization, we extended the ERP to support purchasing and managing pharmacological products. Optum joined as the pilot client and design partner.
Pharmacy purchasing carries requirements that general supply purchasing doesn't. Orders tie back to clinical context, documents can include protected health information, and approvals need tight accountability. Designing with Optum meant we could test these flows against real pilot workflows rather than assumptions.
Clinical context

Orders linked to care

Line details capture physician, RN and DRG code, so purchases can be traced back to the clinical context behind them.

Compliance

Protected documents

Uploads can be flagged as containing ePHI, and flagged files are clearly marked wherever they appear.

Pilot partnership

Built with Optum

Flows were designed and reviewed with Optum as the pilot client, grounding decisions in a real deployment.

Designing every state

A standardized system only holds up if the edge cases are standardized too.
Beyond the happy path, I designed the states users actually run into: empty carts, searches with no results, loading screens, and validation errors. Errors follow one pattern everywhere. A page-level banner says something needs attention, and each affected field is highlighted with its own message.

Reflection

Modernizing an ERP isn't a reskin. It's a decision, screen by screen, about which patterns the whole product will share.

Consistency is a feature

When every grid, form and details page behaves the same, users learn the product once instead of module by module.

A design system needs real workflows

Applying the Premier Design System to dense ERP screens tested the components in ways simpler products never did.

Pilot partners sharpen the work

Designing pharmacy capabilities with Optum kept the work grounded in real requirements, not assumptions.

States are where trust is won

Clear errors, empty states and loading feedback matter most in high-stakes enterprise workflows.
