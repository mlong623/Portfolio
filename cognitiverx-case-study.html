Context

CognitiveRx is Premier's AI-powered pharmacy analytics platform. It automates the intake of wholesaler purchasing data and uses machine learning to tell pharmacy leaders what is coming next.
The product does four jobs. It predicts upcoming price increases so teams can buy ahead. It detects early signals of drug shortages. It tracks margins by comparing cost savings with reimbursement. And it helps health systems plan medication strategy with their caregivers in advance.
The intelligence works. Premier reports that one large Midwestern health system saved more than $450,000 in a single month by buying ahead of predicted price increases. St. Luke's University Health Network, a 13-hospital system, uses it as one place to see its supply chain, from purchasing to price changes.
The interface had not kept pace with the intelligence behind it. Each module had grown on its own, and the result was dense, inconsistent and visually out of step with the rest of Premier's products.

The challenge

Pharmacy buyers make high-dollar decisions from these screens. Every extra panel, label style and control competes with the one number they actually need.
Goal 01

Streamline

Simplify each screen so the predictions and signals lead, and supporting controls step back.

Goal 02

Reduce cognitive load

Remove competing styles, always-open panels and noise so users can scan, compare and decide faster.

Goal 03

Align to the system

Rebuild every screen from Premier Design System components so CognitiveRx looks and behaves like the rest of the portfolio.

How might we let pharmacy teams act on an AI prediction in seconds, without losing any of the data depth they rely on?

Design principles

Four principles turned "reduce cognitive load" into decisions we could apply to every screen.
01

Data first, controls on demand

Filters and settings appear when someone asks for them. The rest of the time, the data gets the full width of the page.

02

Lead with the answer

Each page opens with a short row of KPIs, so the headline is clear before anyone reads a table.

03

One voice, one style

One typeface, sentence case and a single link style replace serif titles, all-caps labels and underlined blue links.

04

Build from the system

Grids, steppers, tabs, cards and buttons come from the Premier Design System, so a pattern learned once works everywhere.

Before and after

Every redesigned screen was set beside its legacy version. Four of them show the changes that mattered most.
Buy-ins

My Predictions

This is where teams act on predicted price increases. The legacy screen kept 13 filter groups open on the left at all times and pushed range filters into the header, leaving the predictions table squeezed in between.

  • Filters moved behind a single Filters button, giving the table the full page width.
  • Two floating KPI cards became one strip of four: buy-in NDCs, impacted facilities, total impact and prediction accuracy.
  • Rows now expand in place, and bulk actions and export sit together above the grid.
Signals & Shortages

National Shortages

A ranked list of problem drugs drives a detail view of fill rate and supply impact. The legacy version mixed serif and sans-serif type, tab styles and all-caps legends, and its charts used inconsistent color.

  • A chart/table toggle and a clear selected state make the problem-drug list easy to scan and switch.
  • Every impact card follows one template, with a chart or list toggle in the same place.
  • Charts use the Premier Design System data palette, with consistent legends in sentence case.
Margin

Margin Modeler

A five-step wizard compares a drug's margin against alternatives. In the legacy form, labels drifted out of line with their inputs, so it was hard to tell which value belonged to which account type.

  • Inputs sit in a true comparison table: one row per account type, one column per drug.
  • Column headers name the primary and comparison drugs and show each one's payment status.
  • Navigation and Cancel, Save & Exit and Next follow the standard design system action bar.
Signals & Shortages

Drug Shortage Alerts

Alerts rank the drugs most at risk. The redesign keeps every data point but trades the side filter panel, mixed card headers and underlined links for a calmer layout.

  • Early detection rate and average lead time sit beside the charts as plain KPI cards.
  • The alert list uses the standard grid, with sortable rank and a quick add-to-watchlist action.
  • Filters, search and export follow the same placement as every other screen.

System-wide changes

The biggest reduction in cognitive load came from consistency. Once a pattern was set, it was applied across all 36 screens.
Navigation

A quieter sidebar

Only the active section expands, with a clear current-page marker, instead of every menu open at once.

Layout

One page frame

Every screen shares the Premier header, a page title with its primary action, and a content card.

Filters

On demand

One filter pattern with Clear and Apply replaces side panels and header range inputs.

Data

Standard grids

Tables share headers, row actions, pagination, rows per page and export placement.

Charts

One palette

Charts use the design system data colors and a consistent chart/table toggle.

Typography

One type system

Sentence case and a single typeface replace serif titles, all-caps labels and underlined links.

Reflection

With AI products, the model is only half the work. The other half is making its answer easy to trust and act on.

Less on screen, not less data

Hiding controls until they're needed cut clutter without removing a single data point users depend on.

SMEs know what matters

Pharmacy experts were the fastest route to knowing which numbers lead a decision and which can wait.

Side by side keeps everyone honest

Placing each new screen beside its legacy version made reviews concrete and showed what had changed at a glance.

Consistency compounds

Each pattern settled on one screen made the next 35 faster to design and easier to learn.
